CORE
01 — Core Architecture

Persistent Core.

The Core contains everything that does not change: flight control, power management, computing, thermal regulation, communication. Standardised, hardened, mass-producible.

Structure

Load-Bearing Carbon Fibre

Not a container — a structural member. Carbon-fibre shear webs carry wing bending moments through the Core. FEA-validated to 6g ultimate load.

Computing

Integrated Intelligence

Flight controller, Mercury AI processor, power management, thermal regulation and mesh comms — all in the standardised Core chassis.

Production

Mass-Production Ready

Identical Core across all units. Standardised SKU wings. Defined assembly stages from raw prepreg to flight-ready, with repeatability targets.

Repair

Field Maintainable

10+ year service life with field repair. Component replacement without depot-level maintenance. Single-technician config swap under three minutes.

QRAM
02 — Mission Interface

QRAM System.

Quick-Release Aerodynamic Mount: the mechanical, electrical and data interface between Persistent Core and mission modules. Field reconfiguration without tools, calibration, or ground support.

Mechanical

Tool-Free Interface

Precision alloy alignment pins with spring-loaded retention. Shear-lug load transfer, 500 N·m capacity, self-aligning within 0.1 mm.

Electrical

48V Power Bus

48V/30A primary, 5V/3A logic. Auto polarity protection. Hot-swap with <100 ms reconnection. Connector-less laser-welded solar cell strings.

Data

Gigabit + CAN Bus

Gigabit Ethernet for Mercury sensor data, CAN bus for control surfaces, USB 3.0 for payload. Auto parameter adaptation on wing recognition.

Propulsion

VTOL by Default

Coaxial propulsion and gimbal vectoring live in the Core, so every wing swap keeps full VTOL — no separate retrofit required.

03 — Propulsion Architecture

Coaxial counter-rotating drive.

Dual counter-rotating motors on a single thrust axis, mounted on a 2-axis gimbal with continuously variable-pitch blades. Counter-rotation cancels reactive torque without a tail rotor — fewer single-point failures, more usable thrust per watt.

Pitch Vectoring

±15–25°

Continuously variable-pitch blades for hover stability and powered transition between vertical lift and forward cruise.

Yaw Vectoring

±10–15°

Gimballed thrust axis provides yaw authority in hover without a tail rotor or reaction wheel — less drag, simpler structure.

04 — Energy Systems
